# 04 · Knaflic's visual models and practical cases

Based on chapters 6 (pp. 166–179), 8 (204–223), and 9 (224–260). The TXT lacks original illustrations; worksheets derive from textual explanations and are not reproductions of figures.

## Five models from chapter 6

1. Lines: evolution, direct labels, reasonable intervals, emphasis on relevant patterns; sufficient scale without requiring legend lookup.
2. Lines with annotations and forecast: separate historical/future data by styles and markers, choose relevant figures and dates, soften secondary elements; clear forecast start.
3. 100% stacked columns: compare composition, highlight priority segment on a common baseline, align title and legend, selective values, denominators.
4. Positive and negative stacked columns: movements in talent supply/demand and unmet needs; emphasize relevant gap, preserve positive/negative axis and labels, do not confuse the sum of components.
5. Stacked bars: survey priorities; order relevant categories, accent the main ones, combine total percentage and breakdown; soften context.

## Chapter 8: integration

Case of price evolution, product C launch in 2010. The general hypothesis that all prices fell is reconsidered after studying differences. Reuse the process, not the conclusion: understand context; try bars and individual/combined lines; declutter; revise message based on data; emphasize different insights; align, title, and annotate; reveal progressively or produce a static version. Do not establish the narrative before analysis.

## Five chapter 9 cases

- Dark background: prefer white where possible; if branding requires dark, soften secondary elements and use high contrast; review projection and print.
- Animation: game user growth; reveal in stages and distinguish current segment from context. Distributed version works without animation through annotations or static views.
- Logical order: user satisfaction; order by satisfaction, dissatisfaction, or non-use depending on the task. If consecutive, stable base and order so the audience learns once, change focus. If standalone, change order where helpful, without bias.
- Spaghetti chart: donation categories, one highlighted line against neutral others; separate panels; or panels with one highlighted series and all others as context. Same X/Y scales and periods if magnitudes are comparable. Declare justified differences.
- Pie alternatives: before/after education survey; bars, paired bars, 100% stacked, or slopegraphs depending on level, composition, or change. Nearby comparisons and common baseline; preserve categories and denominators.

## Redesign procedure

Record values, unit, source, and limits; define task and message; diagnose by impact (misleading, confusing, clutter, aesthetics); try one or two alternatives; maintain data and context, declare filters; apply decluttering and focus, title, annotations, and order; compare what is seen, understood, and actionable; verify numbers. If only an image exists, do not invent hidden values.
