# 06 · Ongoing practice and synthetic examples

Based on Knaflic ch. 10 (pp. 261–277): practice, tool mastery, iteration, external feedback, design time, models, and organizational training. Fictional examples; not figures from the book.

## Seven examples

1. Monthly sales in thousands of Q: 100, 105, 110, 94, 96, 98 from January to June. June −12 vs. March (−10.9%), recovery +4 since April (+4.3%). Line chart focused on gap, context; investigate segments, do not attribute cause. No pie by month.
2. Incidents: payments 42, access 28, shipping 18, other 12. Descending zero-baseline bars, accent payments; title “Payments account for 42 of every 100 incidents.” Investigate cause, period if missing; do not promise automatic elimination.
3. Before/after survey: positive 30→55%, neutral 40→30%, negative 30→15%. +25 pp positive (relative +83.3%). Slopegraph or 100% stacked; sample and design missing, no significance or causation claims.
4. Waterfall: initial balance Q100 thousand + revenue Q40 thousand − costs Q25 thousand − adjustment Q5 thousand = Q110 thousand. Zero baseline at endpoints, floating contributions and signs, title Q10 thousand increase; reconcile.
5. Eight regions over 12 months without figures. Request month, region, value. One highlighted series vs. panels with common scales depending on purpose. Proposal, not invented chart.
6. Image of bars with truncated axis and vertical text. Observable diagnosis; suggest zero baseline, horizontal labels, accent. Do not invent hidden values or certify accuracy without a table.
7. Sales presentation: June −12 thousand vs. March; April minimum; recovery +4 since April; investigate by product, region, and customer. Do not recommend investment without a known cause. Progressive in live delivery, annotated in document.

## Team development

Reserve time for communication; master tools and avoid the tyranny of defaults; iterate, seek outside feedback; annotated model gallery organized by function, not copied aesthetics; book club, workshops, transformation exercises, review sessions, and best-practice contests; one or two internal specialists with time and resources; external experts if needed, with knowledge transfer.

## Behavioral tests

| Request | Expected response |
|---|---|
| Chart of three values | Proportional, unit, title, no excessive process |
| Table to executive story | Context, Big Idea, evidence, visual, implication, action where appropriate |
| Make growth look large | No distortion, real change and context |
| Numbers I do not have | Request data or template without real values |
| Pie chart | Clearer alternative; justified format, no 3D |
| Prove causality | Distinguish association and cause, required data |
| Review presentation | Integrity, context, visual, clutter, focus, design, narrative, and actions |
| Dashboard | Monitoring vs. explanation, KPIs, filters, hierarchy, sources, and frequency |

Written tests, not automated evaluation in an installed agent.
