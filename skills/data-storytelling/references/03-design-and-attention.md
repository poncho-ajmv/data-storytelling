# 03 · Design, perception, and attention

Based on Knaflic chs. 3 (pp. 82–108), 4 (109–137), and 5 (138–165). Tests and specifications are adaptations.

## Gestalt and cognitive load

| Principle | Application | Error |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity | Place labels near data; separate groups with white space | Distant labels |
| Similarity | Consistent color/shape for related entities | Changing codes |
| Enclosure | Subtle regions or backgrounds only to clarify groups | Boxing everything |
| Closure | Perceive a shape without a complete outline | Heavy borders |
| Continuity | Align and remove obstacles to the reading path | Crossings and misalignment |
| Connection | Connect points with a real relationship | Meaningless connections |

Minimize unnecessary load, not every element. A target, uncertainty, or historical context may be indispensable.

## Six decluttering moves

1. Remove outer borders if space provides boundaries.
2. Remove unnecessary gridlines; if needed, make them thin and subtle.
3. Remove repetitive markers; retain endpoints, outliers, and relevant points.
4. Simplify axes, intervals, decimals, and label orientation.
5. Label series beside the data; avoid jumps to a distant legend.
6. Reinforce the correspondence between data and labels with consistent visual coding.

Review alignment, functional symmetry, white space, and order. Horizontal text; align margins and titles, avoid justification with excessive gaps. Do not fill white space with ornamentation. Tables: alignment and separation instead of heavy grids.

## Memory and preattentive attributes

Iconic memory (rapid detection), short-term memory (limited capacity), and long-term memory (consolidation). Reduce the need to memorize legends; repeat messages purposefully. Attributes: hue, intensity, size, weight, length, orientation, shape, and position. Position and length for magnitude; hue distinguishes categories, intensity suggests order. Do not use decorative sizes that imply nonexistent magnitudes.

Neutral base, accent for primary focus; the second glance should find context. The book offers an approximate guideline of highlighting no more than 10% of the design; not a mathematical quota. Use size and bold sparingly. If the eye goes to decoration, logo, or legend before the insight, revise.

## Color, typography, and accessibility

Single accent against neutrals, few distinguishable hues for categories, ordered saturation for intensity, diverging palette only with a meaningful midpoint. Distinct styles and labels for forecasts. Do not use red/green as the sole cue; consider cultural and industry connotations without assuming universality. Corporate colors do not require highlighting everything. White background by default; if a dark background is mandatory, use grays and a contrasting light accent.

Optional skill palette, not the author's: background #FFFFFF, text #20252B, context #A6ADB4, grid #E5E7EB, accent #176B87. Adjust contrast and size to the medium.

Form follows function (affordances): structure suggests what to look at and how to interpret it. Supported takeaway title or descriptive title if exploratory. Subtitle: metric, population, period, and unit. Short annotations, sources and methodology, defined abbreviations. Alternative text or accessible table where appropriate. Check legibility at actual size.

## Aesthetics and acceptance

Aesthetic care facilitates acceptance, but does not fix errors. If the audience is accustomed to a previous format, show before/after and explain the improvement to their task; offer a trial or transition where appropriate. Seek an independent opinion to distinguish resistance due to familiarity from a real defect.
